# Recognising scams and phishing

A bank or card issuer will not ask you for your full PIN, your online banking password or a one-time code by phone, email or text message. Anyone who asks for these is trying to take over your account, even if the caller knows your name or the last digits of your card.

Scammers create urgency: "your account will be closed in one hour", "we have stopped a payment, confirm now". Slow down. Hang up, and call the number printed on your card or shown in your banking app.

Do not click links in unexpected messages and do not install software at a caller's request. Never share a one-time code, even with someone who says they are from the bank. If you already shared information, contact your issuer immediately and change your passwords.
